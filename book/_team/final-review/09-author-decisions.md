## Book-wide decisions
1. **Bold.** STYLE 9 is updated (the editor does this) to allow bold for: defined terms at first use, fixed lead-ins ("A real incident:", "The problem:", "The lesson.", "Where the analogy breaks down:", "Worked example."), and the one-sentence "lesson" that closes an incident story. Do not un-bold those. Do un-bold bold used for emphasis elsewhere, for identifiers (put them in code font) and for UI labels (italic).
2. **Analogy limits.** Every chapter's analogy must end with the separate bold lead-in **Where the analogy breaks down:** as STYLE 8 requires. Chapters 1-10 and 32-36 still put "the analogy breaks down" inside the analogy paragraph: split it out and add the lead-in (text otherwise unchanged). Ch 5 has no analogy: leave it (record why).
3. **Tier skip lines.** Under every Intermediate and Advanced tier heading there is an italic "you can skip" line (STYLE 7). Add it where missing (Ch 1-10); Ch 4 and Ch 5 keep their pacing note if it already says the same thing.
4. **Exercise order.** Do NOT reorder or renumber exercises (Appendix C, hints and references depend on the numbers). Leave a chapter ending on a two-star exercise as it is.
5. **Star time estimates.** Follow How to use this book: one star 5 to 15 minutes, two stars 30 to 60 minutes, three stars an hour or more. Fix Ch 1 (and any chapter that differs).
6. **Serial (Oxford) comma** everywhere in prose, headings, captions and glossary definitions. The editor runs a supervised candidate scan after the owners finish; owners fix the ones they see.
7. **Closing quotation marks.** American style: periods and commas inside the quotation marks. Owners fix the ones they see; the editor scans afterwards.
8. **"recognised"** is now "recognized" everywhere in prose, headings, captions, glossary and Mermaid labels ("recognized device"). Code identifiers and quoted repository text keep their spelling. Backend: Figure 16.1's Mermaid label "Recognised device?" becomes "Recognized device?" (the cache re-renders only that figure). The editor sweeps the rest.
9. **Cross-references.** "Section", "Sections", "Chapter", "Exercise", "Listing", "Example", "Figure", "Table" are capitalized when they name a numbered item. Bare "(37.5)" style references get the word "Section" (owner of Ch 37 and the solutions). Wrong section numbers must be fixed after checking the actual heading.
10. **The AI reviewers.** Name them once in full at first mention in each chapter ("the AI product-owner reviewer", "the AI technical-manager reviewer") and afterwards "the PO reviewer" and "the TM reviewer". The person who owns the project and made the decisions is "the project owner" (never "the product owner"). Apply in Ch 25-37 (app writer: 25-31; production writer: 32-37 and 39-41); the editor applies it in the preface, how-to-use, epilogue and appendices.
11. **Acronyms and terms** are spelled out at first use in each chapter (HSTS, CSP, SPA, OIDC, SSO, MFA, GA, e2e ...). "e2e" is either introduced once ("end-to-end (e2e) tests") or replaced by "end-to-end".
12. **"front end".** Use "frontend" for the Angular app; for Caddy write "HTTPS proxy", not "HTTPS front end".
13. **Text description label.** Keep `*Text description:*` as it is.
14. **Fragment-only lists** keep their periods; do not change them (STYLE 3 is updated by the editor to say full-sentence list items end with a period and fragments do not; the existing lists are left).
15. **Uncaptioned code blocks taken from the repository** (Ch 28.8, 29.11, 30.13 and any others): give them a numbered Listing caption and a Path line, checked against the tag, or mark them as an "Example" if they are written for teaching. Renumber later listings in the chapter and fix references.
16. **Voice.** The author speaks as "I" only where the text already does; otherwise use "you" or the neutral voice. Headings do not use "we" ("Why we test" becomes "Why test" or "Why tests matter"; "the threat we start with" becomes "the threat the project starts with"). "We checked this" (Ch 23.8) becomes a neutral description of the experiment.
